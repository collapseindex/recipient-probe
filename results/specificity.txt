=== Specificity of the steering direction ===
Is the handle the *intent* direction, or a generic feedback/verbosity knob any big push would hit?

Two dissociations (paper Sec. Specificity):

1. ONLY THE DISCRIMINATIVE DIRECTION STEERS (already in Sec. Recover)
   - difference-of-means direction at the peak-probe layer FAILS the sanity gate.
   - logistic-weight (discriminative) direction at a later layer PASSES.
   -> not any intent-correlated axis works; the discriminating direction does.

2. NORM-MATCHED CONTROLS DON'T REPRODUCE THE EFFECT (pinned to the ladder's steer layer)
   S = feedback(toward-evaluate) - feedback(toward-recognize), over 24 items.
   Qwen2.5-3B (L30):  true S=17  (toward-eval 19/24 feedback, toward-rec 2/24)
   Qwen2.5-7B (L16):  true S=18  (toward-eval 20/24, toward-rec 2/24)
   random directions of matched norm: scattered in sign, centered ~0, max |S| = 14 (3B), 16 (7B) -- never reach true.

CAVEAT WE KEEP ON THE RECORD (appendix): shuffled-LABEL permutation
   Qwen-3B: shuffled S in {-3,0,1,1,1,2,3,5,5,6,8,10} (all < 17)          p_shuf=0.077
   Qwen-7B: shuffled S in {-17,-11,-8,-7,-7,-6,-4,2,5,5,13,18}            p_shuf=0.154  <- ONE tied true (18)
   The shuffled control has a FAT TAIL: when the intent axis is strong (7B is the sharpest discard in the
   ladder), an occasional label permutation correlates with true intent and produces a real effect. This is a
   property of shuffled-label permutation on strong effects, NOT evidence the direction is unspecific. We rest
   the specificity claim on the two dissociations above (esp. diff-of-means), not a permutation p-value.

History: first pass (6 items/side, 8 perms, a RE-SWEPT layer L28) was underpowered and on the 3B a shuffled
dir beat true (S 11>9, p=0.22). Fix = pin to the validated ladder layer (L30) + 12 items/side + 12 perms.
The 3B then separated cleanly; the 7B tie is the fat tail, reported honestly rather than chased to p<0.05.
