=== Deflationary control: is late-layer steering just opener-token biasing? ===
Script: experiments/modal_opener_control.py  |  Output: sweep_opener_control.json  |  n=24 (12/side)

The objection: steering the intent direction at a late layer may not "route a discarded intent," it may just bias
the opener-token distribution toward acknowledgment openers (near logit-steering). If so, (a) the intent direction
should align with the (ACK-opener - FB-opener) unembedding direction, (b) steering that opener direction directly
should reproduce the recovery, and (c) the effect should live only in the first token.

Per model, at its validated ladder steer layer, matched norm, same hook as modal_specificity2.py:
  S_true      = feedback(+intent) - feedback(-intent)                     [does intent steering control honoring]
  S_opener    = feedback(+opener) - feedback(-opener)                     [does the opener direction reproduce it]
  S_true_rest = S_true scored on the reply with its FIRST SENTENCE REMOVED [body reorientation vs opening move]
  cos_L       = |cos(intent_dir, opener_dir)| at the steer layer          [are the two axes the same direction]

TWO budgets were run. The specificity section (modal_specificity2.py) uses MAXTOK=45. This control was RE-RUN at
MAXTOK=100 so the first-sentence-removed body test has a body to score (at 45 tokens most replies are one sentence).

  --- MAXTOK=100 (the reported run; body test valid) ---
  model         layer  S_true  S_opener (coh)      S_true_rest  cos_L   rand_maxabs
  Qwen2.5-7B    16     14      -16 (2/24 coh)      14           0.001   11
  Llama-3.1-8B  19      7        1 (24/24 coh)      4           0.034    3
  Qwen2.5-3B    30      4       -1 (0-4/24 coh)    -2           0.138   12

  --- MAXTOK=45 (first run, for reference; matches specificity S_true 14/16/8) ---
  Qwen2.5-3B 14 | Qwen2.5-7B 16 | Llama-3.1-8B 8

READING (mixed, reported straight):
- Qwen-7B = CLEAN REFUTATION, strengthened at 100 tokens. cos=0.001 (intent axis orthogonal to the opener axis).
  S_true_rest=14 EQUALS the full-reply S_true=14: with the first sentence removed, intent steering swings feedback
  just as much, so it reorients the whole response body, not the opening move. Direct opener-direction steering
  cannot reproduce it (breaks coherence at matched norm, 2/24 coherent on the ack side).
- Llama-8B = NOW SUPPORTS (was inconclusive at 45 tokens, where S_true=8 tied rand_maxabs=8). At 100 tokens
  S_true=7 clears rand_maxabs=3, so steering is specific; S_true_rest=4 shows a modest body effect; the opener
  direction is inert (S_opener=1, no separation). Opener-biasing refuted here too.
- Qwen-3B = BODY TEST INCONCLUSIVE, and an honest new finding: at 100 tokens the intent-steering separation
  DILUTES to S_true=4, below rand_maxabs=12. Steered toward acknowledgment, the small model acknowledges first
  but drifts back into feedback over a long reply (neg side feedback 3/24 at 45 tokens -> 17/24 at 100). So the
  steering effect on the smallest model is FRONT-LOADED and washes out over ~100 tokens. The opener-biasing
  refutation on 3B therefore rests on the norm-independent facts: near-orthogonality (cos=0.138) and the opener
  direction's incoherence under steering, NOT on S_rest.

BOTTOM LINE: opener-biasing is refuted on the two models where steering is clean at this budget (Qwen-7B strongly,
Llama-8B on the specificity/orthogonality/inert-opener triad); on Qwen-3B the body test is inconclusive because
the intervention is front-loaded and dilutes over a long generation, and the refutation there rests on
orthogonality + opener incoherence. Caveat on the control: S_opener reuses the intent direction's activation norm,
which over-drives the opener axis and costs coherence; the load-bearing evidence is the norm-independent cosine
(all three) plus the 7B body reorientation, not the opener-steer coherence collapse.
