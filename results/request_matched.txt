=== Construct-validity control: intent, or just request-detection? ===
Script: experiments/modal_request_matched.py  |  Output: sweep_request_matched.json  |  n=120, leave-phrasing-out CV

The objection: in the surface-matched stated-intent stimuli, EVALUATE prefixes carry an explicit directive
("be blunt...") and RECOGNIZE prefixes carry none, so the probe might decode "is a directive present" rather
than the sender's intent. Fix: hold request-presence CONSTANT -- every recognize prefix now also carries an
explicit request (celebrate-with-me / don't-critique), matched to the evaluate side (critique-this). Only the
CONTENT of the request differs. Same surface-matched suffix. If the probe still decodes at ceiling, it reads
what is requested (intent), not whether something is requested.

READING:
- Probe decodes recognize-vs-evaluate at 0.90-1.00 even with request-presence matched. Request-detection is
  REFUTED: the signal is the content of the request (the intent), not the presence of a directive.
- BoW rose from 0.48 (surface-matched original) to 0.69 here, because request-matched recognize phrasings carry
  celebration lexicon ("celebrate", "happy", "proud", "enjoy") absent from the evaluate side -- unavoidable when
  you match request-presence. But the probe clears BoW by ~0.25-0.30 on every model, so lexical cues do not
  account for it.

COMPLEMENTARY-CONTROLS FRAMING (the strong form):
- Surface-matched (original): identical suffix -> BoW at chance 0.48 (controls LEXICAL leakage); recognize lacks
  a directive (request-presence confound present).
- Request-matched (this run): both classes carry directives (controls REQUEST-PRESENCE); BoW rises to 0.69
  (more lexical leakage).
- The probe hits ceiling in BOTH. Design 1 kills the lexical explanation; design 2 kills the request-detection
  explanation; the only thing constant across both designs is that the probe reads intent. Neither single
  confound survives.
